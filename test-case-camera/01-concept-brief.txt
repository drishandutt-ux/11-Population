# "Pip" programmable keychain camera — concept brief (TEST DATA)

This is an invented concept brief written for testing the tool. The company, the product name, the
prices and every figure are made up. Treat them as illustrative.

## The concept, as it would be shown to a respondent

**Pip is a camera the size of a keyring fob that you can teach to do a job.**

Stick it anywhere with the clip, magnet or suction mount in the box. Open the Pip app, connect the
camera, and tell it in plain words what you want it to do. Examples:

- *"Watch my wardrobe and build me a private website, laid out like a shopping site, showing every
  item I own, what is clean, and outfits I could wear today."*
- *"Watch the fridge shelf and tell me what is about to go off, and what I could cook with it."*
- *"Watch the cot while I'm in the next room and tell me if the baby rolls over or the blanket
  covers their face."*
- *"Watch the front door and tell me when a parcel is left, or a person I don't know lingers."*
- *"Watch the dog while I'm out and tell me if he's on the sofa."*

The app picks a starting point from a library of expert-made and open-source vision templates,
then trains a small model for your camera on your own footage over the first day or two. It
learns your wardrobe, your fridge, your baby, not a generic one. You can correct it ("that's a
scarf, not a belt") and it gets better.

## What is in the box and what it costs

| Item | Price (test) |
|---|---|
| One Pip camera (1080p, wide lens, night vision, magnetic back, USB-C, 3-day battery or plug-in) | £49 |
| Three-pack | £119 |
| Mount kit (clip, suction cup, adhesive plate, fridge-shelf arm, cot-rail clamp) | £9 |
| Pip app: connect cameras, set tasks, train, alerts | free |
| **Pip Plus** (optional): the shop-style wardrobe website, sharing with family, 30-day event history, unlimited retraining | £3.99 a month, cancel any time |

Everything the camera sees is processed on the camera and your phone. Footage never leaves your
home unless you turn on Pip Plus history.

## What the company wants to know

1. Do people want a camera you *programme*, or do they just want another security camera?
2. Which job do they set it up to do first, and which job would they pay for?
3. Is £49 the right price for one camera, and will anyone pay £3.99 a month for Plus?
4. What stops people between "interested" and "using it every day"?
5. Does "all on-device, nothing leaves your home" persuade the privacy-worried, or do they not believe it?

## What the company can change (levers)

- The price of a camera, the three-pack and the Plus plan.
- Which templates ship at launch (wardrobe, fridge, baby, doorstep, pet, plants, garage, workshop).
- A "no cloud, ever" mode with the Plus website served from a box in the home instead of online.
- Setup that takes under five minutes, with the first task chosen from a picture menu.
- A "show me exactly what it can see and what it keeps" screen in the app.
- A trade-in for an old security camera.

## What the company cannot change quickly

- Battery life: three days on battery, or plugged in.
- The camera needs Wi-Fi at home and a phone made in the last five years.
- Training takes a day or two of footage before the task works well.
- It is a camera. Some people will never put one in a bedroom or bathroom, whatever the promise.
