# The six use-case cards (TEST DATA)

Short stimulus cards. Each is shown to a respondent on its own. Used in the Lab as the material
for the survey, the A/B test and the message test. Prices as in the concept brief.

## Card A — Wardrobe shop
Clip Pip inside the wardrobe door. Over two days it learns every item you own. The app then gives
you a private website laid out like a shopping site: everything you have, what is clean, what you
have not worn in months, and three outfits for today's weather. Needs Pip Plus (£3.99 a month) for
the website; the app alone still lists what you own.

## Card B — Fridge and food
Stick Pip to the fridge-shelf arm. It learns what goes in and out, and tells you what is about to
go off and what you could cook with it tonight. No subscription needed.

## Card C — Baby in the next room
Clamp Pip to the cot rail. It tells you if the baby rolls onto their front, if a blanket covers
their face, or if they have been crying for more than a minute. Everything is processed on the
camera and your phone. Nothing is uploaded, ever, unless you switch on history.

## Card D — Doorstep and security
Magnet Pip to the door frame. It tells you when a parcel is left, when it is taken, and when a
person it does not recognise lingers for more than thirty seconds. Faces of people you tag as
family are never alerted.

## Card E — Pet at home
Put Pip on the shelf. It tells you if the dog is on the sofa, if the cat has been sick, or if the
water bowl is empty. Sends a clip to your phone.

## Card F — Older relative living alone
Put Pip in the hallway of a parent's home. It tells you if they have not been seen by ten in the
morning, or if they have been on the floor for more than a minute. Only they and you can see
anything; the camera never records, it only checks.
