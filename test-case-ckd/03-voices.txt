# Voices from the two pilots (TEST DATA — invented quotes)

Fourteen short comments gathered during the Eastfield and Colliery Row pilots. Names, places and
details invented. Professionals speak for the people they see; patients speak for themselves.

1. **Dr Helen, GP partner, Eastfield** — "I have nine minutes and a room full of things that are actually hurting. A silent kidney number is never going to win that fight unless someone else does the follow-up."
2. **Amrit, clinical pharmacist, Colliery Row network** — "Half the abnormal results I see have sat in a workflow for a month. If I could start the treatment at the same conversation as the result, we'd stop losing people between the two."
3. **Jodie, pharmacy technician, Eastfield** — "A letter goes in the drawer. A phone call from someone local who says 'we've got you a slot Thursday at six' gets people in. I did forty calls; twenty-three came."
4. **Brian, 64, Colliery Row, ex-miner** — "Nobody's ever told me my sugar could do my kidneys in. I'd have come years ago if they'd said that."
5. **Fatima, 52, Eastfield, cleaner** — "I can't take a morning off for a test that I'm told I don't need. Do it at the chemist on the way home and I'm there."
6. **Dr Raj, consultant nephrologist** — "I meet people for the first time with an eGFR of twelve. They had four abnormal results in primary care that nobody coded. The programme is aimed at exactly that."
7. **Kevin, 58, Eastfield, taxi driver** — "Started the water tablet, was up three times a night, stopped it. Nobody rang. That was it."
8. **Margaret, 71, Colliery Row** — "The letter said 'chronic kidney disease' and I thought that was it, dialysis. A nurse explained it on the phone and I calmed down. Should've been explained first."
9. **Sunita, 45, Eastfield, shop worker** — "I'm not on benefits, so it's £9.90 a box. Two boxes a month for something I can't feel? I stretched it to one."
10. **Paul, ICB commissioner** — "I need one number I can put in front of the board: how many more people on protective treatment at twelve months, and whether the gap between our poorest and richest wards closed or opened."
11. **Grace, community outreach worker, Eastfield** — "In the Somali and Bangladeshi households I go into, the men won't do a urine sample at the surgery. A kit at home changes that completely."
12. **Dr Tom, GP, Colliery Row** — "The protocol is fine. My worry is who owns the patient at month four when the pharmacist's contract ends and the review is due."
13. **Lorraine, 66, Colliery Row** — "Mobile clinic was in the Co-op car park. I did it because I was there anyway. I wouldn't have gone to the surgery for it."
14. **Dev, 39, Eastfield, warehouse** — "I've got diabetes, I know. I don't want another appointment telling me things I already feel guilty about."
