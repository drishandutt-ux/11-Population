People in a northern English integrated care system (former coalfield towns and a coastal town,
high deprivation, some long-settled South Asian and newer Somali and Eastern European communities)
who are involved in a kidney-check programme. Mostly patients: adults aged 40 to 80 on the diabetes,
blood-pressure or heart-disease registers who have not had a full kidney check in the last year,
across the deprivation range but weighted to the poorest fifth, owners and renters, in work, retired
and out of work, some who never open NHS letters, some who attend everything, some who have been
started on a tablet before and stopped it. Plus the professionals who see them: GP partners with no
spare appointments, clinical pharmacists and pharmacy technicians in the primary care networks,
community pharmacists, one nephrologist, one ICB commissioner, community outreach workers.
Professionals answer for the people they serve. Ordinary people, not analysts.
