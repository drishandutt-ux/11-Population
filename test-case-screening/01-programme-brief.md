# Screening at home — programme brief (TEST SCENARIO)

*This brief is a test scenario written for a tool demonstration. The national programme and every
figure in `02-facts` are real and public; the working group, its options and its decisions are
invented.*

## The situation

Cervical screening in England checks for high-risk HPV, the virus behind almost all cervical
cancers. About two in three eligible women are up to date; about one in three is not, and
participation has drifted down for a decade. The NHS has committed to eliminating cervical cancer
by 2040.

On 25 August 2026 NHS England began inviting women who have not come forward for screening to
order a **free home self-sampling kit**: a swab taken at home, posted back free, and tested for
high-risk HPV. The first phase covers people aged 30 to 65 who have not attended after earlier
invitations, around four million women. Anyone whose kit finds high-risk HPV is invited to a
follow-up appointment at a GP surgery or sexual health clinic, where a clinician examines the
cervix.

## The question the working group is asking

A women's health working group (test scenario) wants to know, before it puts money behind any
local support for the roll-out:

1. **Who will order and send back a kit**, and who still will not, even with a kit at home?
2. **Where do people drop off** between the invitation and the follow-up appointment after a
   positive result — and how many women sit behind each gap?
3. **Is the gap wider** in the most deprived areas and among Black, South Asian and other ethnic
   minority women, who are already less likely to be screened?
4. **What one change** would move the most women through?
5. **What outcome** should the group commit to, so it can be checked against the real roll-out in
   twelve months?

## The path, as the working group sees it

1. **Invited** — receives an invitation by NHS App, text, email or letter to order a kit.
2. **Read it and understood it** — opens the invitation and knows what is being offered and why.
3. **Ordered a kit** — orders it through the NHS App or an NHS online account.
4. **Took the sample and posted it back** — does the swab and returns it in the prepaid envelope.
5. **Got the result and understood it** — receives the result and knows what it means.
6. **Went to the follow-up** — if HPV was found, books and attends the examination.

## What the group could change (the levers)

- **How the kit is offered:** the NHS App message alone; a text with a one-tap order link; a kit
  handed over in person at any GP or pharmacy visit; a kit posted without being asked for.
- **Where the kit can be picked up:** posted home only, or also over the pharmacy counter.
- **The instructions:** a printed leaflet, a 60-second video, translated versions, easy-read.
- **The result letter:** the current clinical wording, or a plain-language letter with a named
  nurse to call.
- **The follow-up appointment:** standard booking; a female clinician guaranteed; evening and
  Saturday slots; a call within a week to book it for them.

## What cannot change

- The test itself, the age range and the national eligibility rules.
- A positive self-sample still needs an in-person examination; a kit cannot replace it.
- GP appointment capacity in the short term.
- The NHS remains the sender of every invitation and result.

## What the group will do with the answer

Pick at most two local changes for year one, decide which invitation and result-letter wording
to support, and freeze one measurable outcome to check against the real numbers.
