# Reality check — keep this file OUT of the session

*Do not upload this file. It holds a real, published trial result the twins have not been shown,
so the demo can compare what the population predicts with what actually happened.*

## The trial

A randomised trial in West London GP practices, led by Queen Mary University of London with
King's College London, published in *eClinicalMedicine* on 29 July 2025. Women overdue for
cervical screening were offered a self-sampling kit in different ways:

| How the kit was offered | Share who returned a sample |
|---|---|
| Handed over in person during an unrelated GP appointment (234 of 449) | **52%** |
| Kit posted to the home without being asked for | **12%** |
| Letter offering a kit (no kit enclosed) | **5%** |

The earlier YouScreen trial in north and east London (2021, over 27,000 women offered a kit, 8,838
returned) found the same order, and about six in ten of those who returned a kit were from
deprived areas and from ethnic minority groups.

## What to compare in the demo

1. **The order.** In the Test messages step (Pro Step 6.9) and the Choose between / A/B steps,
   the in-person offer (Invitation C) should beat the text (B) and the NHS App message (A). A
   correct order — in person first, by a wide margin — is the headline moment for the client.
2. **The size of the gap.** The real gap between in-person and posted is roughly four to one.
   Twins usually compress gaps (they are more agreeable than real people), so expect the order to
   be right and the gap to be smaller. Say so; it is the honest framing.
3. **Who it reaches.** The trial reached deprived and ethnic minority women well. The Journey's
   equity line at *Invited → Ordered a kit* should show whether the twins agree.

## Caveat

Research is on in both the Simple view and the Pro pass, so the search may find these trials and
the twins may read them. That is fine for a client demo (the tool found the evidence). For a clean
blind check, use the optional research-off pass at the end of the script.
