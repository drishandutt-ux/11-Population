## The home kit

| Fact | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Recommendation | UK National Screening Committee recommended self-sampling for under-screened people | UK NSC, March 2025 |
| Announcement | Department of Health and Social Care | 24 June 2025 |
| Invitations began | 25 August 2026, phased across England, by invitation only | NHS England |
| How it is offered | NHS App, text, email or letter; ordered through the NHS App or NHS online account | NHS England |
| What it is | a vaginal swab taken at home, posted back free, tested for high-risk HPV | NHS England |
| After a positive kit | invited to a follow-up examination at a GP surgery or sexual health clinic | NHS England |
| What it cannot do | a self-sample cannot detect cell changes; it is a first step, not a replacement | Cancer Research UK; The Eve Appeal |
| Experience in the HPValidate study (self-sampling at screening sites) | 75% rated it excellent, 23% good | Cancer Research UK, June 2025 |
| Screening interval change | from 1 July 2025, people aged 25–49 with no HPV are invited every five years instead of three | NHS England |

## Who is under-screened, and why

| Fact | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Groups less likely to be screened | deprived areas, ethnic minority backgrounds, LGBTQ+ people, people with learning disabilities, survivors of sexual violence | Cancer Research UK, June 2025 |
| Reasons given by women who delayed or missed screening (915 women) | embarrassment 72%; uncomfortable with a stranger examining an intimate area 69%; scared it would hurt 58%; did not know what would happen 37%; fear of being judged 18% | Jo's Cervical Cancer Trust survey, 2019 |
| Other barriers in a UK academic survey (194 women aged 25–64) | pain and discomfort, lack of time, embarrassment; about a third cited difficulty making an appointment and finding childcare | University of Leeds |
| Reasons named by NHS clinicians | embarrassment, discomfort, lack of time | NHS England North West, August 2026 |
