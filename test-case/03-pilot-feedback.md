# Loop pilot — user interview notes (30 riders, 6-week Hackney pilot)

Verbatim quotes, lightly cleaned. Interviewer notes in brackets.

**Priya, 31, NHS physiotherapist, Homerton.** "I did the maths in week one —
I was paying £171 for the Tube and hating every minute of it. £65 and I arrive
at work awake. I'm not going back." [Converted, evangelising to colleagues.]

**Dan, 44, black-cab driver's son, drives a Golf, Leyton.** "Sixty-five quid a
month forever? I could BUY a bike for that in three years. Subscriptions are
how they get you. It's Netflix for bikes and I already resent Netflix."
[Hard no. Ownership identity runs deep.]

**Amara, 27, junior designer, Peckham.** "The theft thing is the whole
product for me. My last bike got nicked outside Peckham Rye in broad daylight
and I cried for a week. Knowing it's THEIR problem changed how I park."
[Theft cover = emotional product, not financial.]

**Tom, 52, facilities manager, Islington.** "The bike's fine. The app kept
logging me out. And when I called support I got a chatbot. For £65 I want a
human." [Service expectations scale with price.]

**Jess, 38, mum of two, Walthamstow.** "The cargo bike was life-changing for
the school run, but £95 is dinner-party money. My husband said no. If it were
£70 I'd have fought harder." [Cargo tier: desire high, price ceiling lower.]

**Marcus, 24, bike courier, Hackney Wick.** "It's a solid bike but I ride 200k
a week — the swap guarantee is the only reason I'd pay. Couriers will thrash
these. You should charge us more, honestly." [Heavy-use segment breaks the
economics; interesting.]

**Helen, 61, retired teacher, Stoke Newington.** "I hadn't cycled in twenty
years. The motor makes the hills disappear. But I'm frightened of the
junction at Dalston. It's not the bike, it's the roads." [Infrastructure
fear, not product fear — recurring in the over-50s.]

**Interviewer synthesis:** the pilot splits into (a) TfL refugees doing
cost-per-month maths, (b) theft-anxious ex-cyclists for whom the cover is
emotional, (c) ownership-identity sceptics who will never subscribe, and
(d) infrastructure-fearful would-be riders the product can't reach alone.
NPS of the 30: +41. Cancellations in pilot: 3 (one moved, two "just didn't
ride enough to justify it").
