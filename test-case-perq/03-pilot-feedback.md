# Perq pilot — user interview notes (2 employers, 6-week London pilot)

Verbatim quotes, lightly cleaned. Interviewer notes in brackets.

**Ayesha, 28, marketing executive, Shoreditch fintech.** "I plan my Thursday
around the map now. Flat white deal at 8, free yoga taster at 6, and last
week two stalls tickets for £11 each at 5pm for a 7.30 show. My flatmate
thinks I work in theatre." [Deal-maximiser; opened the app 26 times in week
four. The map is the habit loop.]

**Rob, 46, facilities team, law firm.** "Ten quid a month per head? Times
nine hundred people? Just put it in my payslip. I don't want a fun app, I
want the £120 a year." [Hard pay-not-perks cynic; never activated. Estimates
30% of his floor thinks the same.]

**Tunde, 33, backend engineer, Shoreditch fintech.** "The deals are
genuinely good, that's the problem. An app my employer pays for, watching
where I am at 9pm on a Saturday? No thanks. I'd use it tomorrow if location
never left my phone and they said so in plain English." [Privacy blocker is
specific and fixable — on-device processing + plain-language promise.]

**Leah, 36, catering supervisor, NHS trust, night shifts.** "The surplus
thing is the only perk I've ever actually used. Coming off a late, a £3 bag
from the sushi place that's £14 in the day — that's real money to me. The
theatre stuff is useless at my hours though." [Surplus = killer feature for
shift workers; events skew office-hours.]

**Sam, 24, junior analyst, law firm.** "I got a spa day in the Drop and
traded it for Arctic Monkeys tickets in about four minutes. Best thing any
job has ever given me. Though Priti in my team got nothing two months
running and she's gone off the whole thing." [Drops: euphoric winners,
grumbling non-winners. Fairness perception needs design.]

**Maria, 52, executive assistant, law firm.** "The concierge found an
emergency locksmith for one of the partners at 11pm and a birthday table
nobody could get. But I tried it for myself and felt like I was wasting its
time." [Concierge lands as an exec tool; non-execs self-exclude.]

**David, 58, senior accountant, NHS trust.** "I don't do apps. The lounge
pass I use — that's a proper perk, that says something. Discount pizza on a
map doesn't." [Status perks over deal-hunting in the over-55s; low app
tolerance.]

**Grace, 39, HR director, Shoreditch fintech (the buyer).** "Perkbox gave me
11% monthly active and the CFO noticed. Perq's pilot gave me 47% and people
mention it in exit-interview reversals. If that number holds, £10 is cheap.
If it drops to 20% after the novelty, I'm paying double for the same
non-usage." [The buyer's whole case is sustained activation, not features.]

**Interviewer synthesis:** the pilot splits into (a) map-driven
deal-maximisers, (b) surplus-driven shift workers doing real-money maths,
(c) privacy-wary engineers who like the product but not the observer,
(d) pay-not-perks cynics who will never activate, and (e) status
traditionalists who want the lounge, not the app. Drops divide opinion —
winners evangelise, repeat non-winners sour. Pilot monthly active: 47%.
Surplus add-on attach rate: 31% of activated users.
