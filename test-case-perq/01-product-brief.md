# Perq — Internal Product Brief (v0.7, confidential)

## What Perq is

Perq is an **AI-powered employee incentive platform** sold to employers at
**£10 per employee per month**. The base bundle covers the table stakes every
perks platform offers — retail and gym discounts, exclusive negotiated
discounts, airport & city lounge access, and perks-style insurance cashback
(dental, optical, therapy). Where Perq differs is everything on top:

1. **The Map.** A live map of every offer near the employee right now — our
   negotiated exclusives *and* global/public deals the AI scrapes and
   verifies — so lunch, drinks, gifts and errands route through Perq.
2. **Perq Surplus** (employee add-on, **£3.99/month**). End-of-day surplus
   from bakeries, sushi bars, florists and grocers at 70–90% off or free,
   plus **unfilled event seats** — theatre, comedy, gigs, sports — bought in
   bulk hours before curtain and released to members at up to 80% off with
   real-time registration.
3. **Perq Concierge** (premium tier, **£25/employee/month**, typically
   employer-funded for managers). AI-first concierge with human escalation,
   for emergencies (locksmith, childcare fall-through, travel meltdown) and
   leisure (tables, tickets, trips).
4. **Perq Drops.** Random allocation of big-ticket rewards — a weekend away,
   Premier League tickets, a spa day, new headphones — landing on a lottery
   basis across each employer's workforce every month. Drops are **tradeable
   on the in-app marketplace**: swap your spa day for someone's gig tickets.
5. **The Feed.** A hyper-personalised what-to-buy feed built from saves,
   redemptions and location patterns.

## Why now

- UK employers spend heavily on perks platforms with famously low usage —
  most employees never open the app after onboarding week.
- The surplus economy is proven consumer behaviour (Too Good To Go), but no
  one has bundled it into an employer benefit.
- Venues routinely eat 20–25% unsold inventory; they will discount hard for
  guaranteed bulk purchase the same afternoon.
- Post-pay-freeze years: HR teams need visible, felt value per pound.

## Competitive landscape

| Competitor | Model | Price | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perkbox | Employer perks platform | ~£4–8.50 pepm | Generic vouchers, low monthly usage |
| Ben / Juno | Flexible benefits wallet | ~£8–15 pepm | Budget admin tool, nothing to open daily |
| Too Good To Go | Consumer surplus bags | £4–6/bag | Consumer app, no employer channel, food only |
| TodayTix / rush apps | Last-minute tickets | Per ticket | Theatre only, no bulk-buy guarantee |
| Amex Platinum / Quintessentially | Concierge | £650–£5k+/yr | Individual luxury price point |

Perq's positioning: **"The perk that actually gets used."** The map and the
surplus drops give a reason to open the app on a random Tuesday; everything
else monetises the habit.

## Launch plan

Soft launch with three London employers (a 300-person Shoreditch fintech, a
900-person law firm, a 1,400-person NHS trust catering/estates division),
Zones 1–2 offer density first. Surplus venue supply signed: 60 food outlets,
4 theatres, 2 comedy clubs, 1 arena partner.

## Open questions for the panel

1. Will employees open an employer-provided perks app **weekly**, and which
   single feature drags them back — the map, the surplus food, the tickets,
   or the Drops?
2. Is the location-aware map a delight or a privacy problem when the app is
   linked to your employer?
3. Will employees pay £3.99/month of their **own** money for Surplus on top
   of an app their employer already pays for?
4. Do tradeable random Drops read as exciting and fair — or as workplace
   gambling and favouritism?
5. How loud is the "just put it in my salary" objection, and does it sink
   HR's willingness to pay £10 pepm?
6. Is Concierge an exec-only feature, or does the emergency use case
   (locksmith, childcare) make it mass-market?
